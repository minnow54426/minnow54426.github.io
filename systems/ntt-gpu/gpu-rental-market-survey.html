GPU rental market: where to run this project¶

Checked November 13, 2026 · single-GPU CUDA development · N=65536

Start with a short, on-demand RTX 5090 session. Compare RunPod Community Cloud with verified Vast.ai offers, then choose the machine that supports our toolchain and profiling work at the lowest complete session cost. Keep an RTX 4090 as a compatibility control. H100/H200/B200 rentals become useful when a measured workload justifies their capacity or performance.

This is a primary-source market survey, with a read-only marketplace sample. No machine was booked or tested. Prices are observations, not reserved capacity; the exact configuration and checkout total decide what a session will cost.

What we are renting for¶

Our first paid session verifies the environment and runs the prepared native CUDA checks. The offline preparation is complete; the D11 hardware gate remains open. Learning, documentation, host testing and most build preparation can continue on a CPU.

Requirement Project choice
Primary GPU One full RTX 5090, 32 GB, sm_120
Control GPU One full RTX 4090, 24 GB, sm_89
Workload Exact u64 modular arithmetic and N=65536 transforms; later OpenFHE Hybrid operations
Environment Linux x86_64, SSH, C++20 compiler, compatible driver and CUDA development toolkit
Profiling Compute Sanitizer; Nsight Compute performance-counter access when profiling is scheduled
Practical build allowance About 8 vCPUs, 16–32 GB host RAM and 40–80 GB disk as a starting recommendation

The CPU/RAM/disk allowance is our planning estimate, not a measured minimum. More host resources can save paid compilation time. A runtime-only CUDA image may lack nvcc; choose a development image or install the toolkit. RTX 5090 compilation needs CUDA 12.8 or newer, and the driver must support the toolkit actually used: see NVIDIA's architecture/toolkit matrix. Our offline build used CUDA 13.0; that does not establish a rental's compatibility.

Use the hardware assessment for GPU specifications. Tensor FLOPS, image-generation throughput and LLM tokens per second do not rank our modular kernels. A MIG slice or a different regional GPU variant should not silently replace the named full-GPU baseline.

How the market is divided¶

Rental model What matters for us
Host marketplace, such as Vast.ai Variable price, location, CPU allocation, driver and host reliability; inspect each offer
GPU containers, such as RunPod Pods and AutoDL containers Convenient environments; host-controlled driver and profiling permissions still matter
GPU virtual machines, such as TensorDock and Jarvislabs VM More operating-system control; inspect passthrough, resources and counter permissions
Data-center GPU clouds, such as Lambda Useful for later HBM/capacity comparisons; select the actual instance size
Serverless endpoints and inference APIs A different product; exclude their rates from an interactive CUDA development comparison

Choose on-demand for the first debugging session. Interruptible/spot capacity can make sense later for repeatable jobs that save results and tolerate restart. Monthly reservations are premature while our GPU use is intermittent. Renting also avoids buying a workstation before we know sustained utilization.

The query requested one RTX 5090 or RTX 4090, verified/rentable hosts, on-demand access, reported reliability at least 0.99, CUDA capability at least 13.0, at least 16,000 in the API's host-RAM field, 80 GB available disk and one direct port. Returned records were checked against those filters.

Vast's pricing documentation explains host-set pricing and separate compute, storage and bandwidth charges. Host verification and a reliability score do not establish Nsight permission or guarantee uptime. Requery with your region and full resources before renting.

AutoDL and Lambda: why no current rate is quoted¶

AutoDL is worth checking if its network locations and CNY payment options suit you. Its market was not verified to a specific current offer in this survey. Compare the exact GPU name, CPU/RAM, disk and driver; treat 4090D/5090D labels as distinct configurations rather than substituting them into a 4090/5090 result. Keep CNY and USD quotes separate until you have a dated exchange rate and payment fees.

Lambda offers GPU instances, but its public table explicitly takes effect November 16, 2026 at 00:00 UTC, three days after this survey. It directs users to their account for the current price. The table also defaults to eight-GPU instances while quoting per GPU-hour: multiply by the actual GPU count. Future rates are not used in today's cost comparison.

Provider fit and tradeoffs¶

RunPod — convenient first candidate. Community's 5090 card is an affordable starting point with a documented environment workflow. Secure costs more; compare its specific location and service offering against your needs. Confirm the selected Pod's resources and profiling support before paying.

Vast.ai — price-sensitive alternative. The observed 5090 compute floor was lower than RunPod's public Community card, while the sample median was higher. Select an individual host, not just a GPU label. A low floor does not establish the cheapest usable session after disk, transfer and setup time.

TensorDock — 4090 VM candidate. Its model page describes on-demand KVM VMs with root access and GPU passthrough, and explicitly allows 1–8 GPUs. The page also retains old Q1 2024 wording; its $0.37 floor is not a verified live cart. Get a whole-VM quote with sufficient build resources.

AutoDL — conditional regional candidate. Consider it if access and payment are convenient. Verify a specific market offer and profiler permissions; this survey establishes neither a current hourly GPU price nor Nsight availability.

Jarvislabs — later capacity comparison. Its 96 GB RTX PRO card is a concrete option if our measured working set outgrows 32 GB. Its VM product documents SSH and root access; confirm that the selected product and region match the quoted configuration.

Lambda — later data-center comparison. Useful when the experiment requires HBM or a named data-center GPU. Obtain the current one-GPU or multi-GPU quote; do not pay for eight devices to run a single-device correctness check.

AWS, Azure and Google Cloud also belong in an enterprise procurement comparison. Their region, quota, instance size and attached-resource pricing need a separate matched quote. This shortlist targets our first small CUDA session, rather than claiming complete coverage or a global cheapest provider.

The cost beyond GPU-hours¶

Use one currency and the whole instance rate:

\[ C_{\mathrm{session}} = r_{\mathrm{instance}}t_{\mathrm{billable}} + C_{\mathrm{disk}} + C_{\mathrm{transfer}} + C_{\mathrm{tax/fees}}. \]

Billable time includes environment setup and rebuilding dependencies, not only the seconds spent inside a kernel. Add separately charged CPU/RAM to the instance rate. An account top-up is prepaid balance, not automatically the cost of this experiment. Check region, tax, minimum credit and billing granularity on the final quote.

Billing issue What the official sources establish
RunPod Pods Per-second billing; no ingress/egress fee; one hour's configuration credit needed to deploy on-demand
RunPod retained volume $0.10/GB/month running and $0.20/GB/month stopped; terminating the Pod removes its attached volume disk
RunPod network volume Separately retained storage: $0.07/GB/month below 1 TB; remove it separately when finished
Vast.ai Compute, disk and bandwidth are separately priced; stopping halts compute but keeps disk charges
TensorDock Stopping and unreserving the GPU changes an on-demand server to storage-only pricing; verify reserved resources
AutoDL On-demand billing is per second with a CNY 0.01 minimum; paid disk expansion can remain billable while powered off
Jarvislabs Paused instances retain storage charges; deleting compute does not necessarily remove separately created storage

Export reports before deleting resources. Check the billing/resource view after cleanup, including independent volumes, snapshots and reserved IPs where used.

For a first attempt, a $10–$15 spending allowance is a reasonable planning choice for one short 5090 session plus modest extras. It is neither a provider minimum deposit nor an upper bound: a long build, retained disks, transfer or a different quote can exceed it. Record actual costs and choose the next allowance from observed session time.

At the RunPod Community card rates, 5090 compute costs about 2.03× the 4090. The 5090 must finish a fixed job over 2.03× faster to win on compute cost alone. That speedup is unmeasured; development value and extra VRAM are separate reasons to choose it. Rank later providers by dollars per verified workload, including transfers, rather than dollars per advertised GPU-hour.

Profiling access can decide the provider¶

A machine can run CUDA correctly and still reject Nsight Compute's hardware counters with ERR_NVGPUCTRPERM. NVIDIA's permission guide explains the host/driver permissions and container capabilities involved. SSH access or a root shell inside a container does not itself establish those permissions.

Counter access is unverified for every specific offer in this survey. Ask whether Nsight Compute hardware-counter collection is permitted on the chosen GPU and product, and whether the provider enables the required host permissions. A full VM is worth considering when container restrictions block the experiment, but it also needs verification. A successful nvidia-smi is not that verification.

Treat correctness, sanitizer coverage and profiling as separate observed results. If a host cannot provide counters, it may still serve the correctness session; schedule profiling on a host that can. Do not report the profiling gate complete from timing-only runs.

Recommended first rental¶

  1. Finish preparation on CPU. Use the pinned source/dependencies and the offline handoff. Check image compatibility before copying binaries; rebuilding OpenFHE can consume paid time.
  2. Compare two live carts. Select one full on-demand RTX 5090 on RunPod Community and a suitable verified Vast.ai host. Match region, driver, CPU/RAM, an explicit disk allocation and the complete hourly total.
  3. Resolve profiling access. Obtain a clear answer for the chosen product before a counter-dependent session. Confirm SSH and development tools too.
  4. Run the prepared collection path. Follow the handoff's gpu_session.py command with the actual CUDA and OpenFHE paths. Capture environment, native exactness, sanitizer and benchmark reports. The runner is not the complete later profiling/failure-test suite.
  5. Export evidence and end billing. Save logs and cost records, then stop or delete according to the provider's resource rules. Perform analysis offline.

The next evidence we need is one successful, reproducible native GPU session. Keep D11 open until that evidence exists. Revisit 96 GB cards or H100/H200/B200 after measuring residency and complete-workload performance.
