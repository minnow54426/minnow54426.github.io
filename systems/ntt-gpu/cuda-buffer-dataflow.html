3. Why a polynomial has several copies

CKKS uses a residue number system (RNS) to represent large coefficients. Each copy of the polynomial stores its remainders under a different prime. In this project, one copy is called a tower. For example, the coefficient 20 appears as 3 in the tower modulo 17 and as 20 in the tower modulo 41.

A tower with 65,536 eight-byte values takes 512 KiB. Three towers take 1.5 MiB for one polynomial. Try changing the number of towers below and watch the storage count.
