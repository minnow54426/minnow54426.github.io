Read the NTT evaluation and inverse formulas¶

Outcome¶

Expanded exact evaluation and inverse formulas for beginners. The section now explains the symbols, distinguishes coefficient indices from stored evaluation positions, and works through a complete forward and inverse example. The transform contract and implementation are unchanged.

Reasoning¶

The compact formulas assumed that readers already knew polynomial evaluation, fixed-width bit reversal and modular inverses. Explain each operation before combining them. A four-point example makes the stored ordering visible without requiring a reader to follow a 65536-element calculation.

Keep the production formulas at N=65536 and label the small example explicitly. Put the cancellation proof in optional notes, so readers can follow the calculation before studying why it works.

Changes¶

  • Added a symbol table for coefficients, memory indices, root indices, roots, bit reversal and N-inverse.
  • Explained why odd powers are roots of x^N+1 and mapped the first five memory positions to their natural point indices and root exponents.
  • Read forward as polynomial substitution and inverse as a weighted sum with modular normalization. Distinguished negative powers from array indices and decimal reciprocals.
  • Added N=4, q=17, psi=2 with coefficients [1,2,3,4], stored evaluations [15,11,13,16], inverse roots [9,8,15,2] and recovered coefficients.
  • Added an optional geometric-sum explanation and retained the existing diagnostic probes, upstream source citations and section anchor.

Verification¶

It recovers the original coefficients. The inverse weighted sums modulo 17 are [4,8,12,16]; scaling by 4-inverse, 13, returns [1,2,3,4].

Follow-up: derive the butterfly invariant from the negacyclic merge¶

Expanded the butterfly invariant with the factorization of x^(2d)−β into x^d−α and x^d+α, where α²=β. The child remainders U±αV explain the default group-twiddle butterfly, its per-stage polynomial meaning, its final bit-reversed evaluations and its canonical stored range. Reversing a merge gives 2u and 2v, which explains the single final N-inverse normalization.

CKKS Fourier-extension functional bootstrapping¶

Reviewed the tagged OpenFHE 1.6.1 implementation and the EUROCRYPT 2026 abstract. In the slots-to-coefficients-first path, a complex exponential cancels integer overflows from modulus raising. The Fourier polynomial then evaluates a function of each real input slot. The single-call API accepts coefficients for ReLU as well as smooth functions. A finite series rounds the corner of hard ReLU.

Added the implementation study to Research. It records APIs, admissible functions, ReLU coefficients and the local CPU comparison against ordinary bootstrapping. The comparison times whole calls and excludes context setup, key generation and decryption.

On a single-threaded M1 Pro with 1,024 slots, all six modes left eight effective levels. The median ordinary bootstrap was 10.76 s. The three official smooth-function examples took 13.46–17.59 s, while 45- and 129-degree ReLU took 14.24 s and 20.54 s. The corresponding ReLU maximum absolute errors were 0.0571 and 0.0127 over the tested grid, matching the plaintext Fourier evaluation at the displayed precision. These are diagnostic timings under one parameter set, with three measured samples per mode and without an independent security estimate. All 27 documentation tests passed, and the site build checked 42 pages.

Next research comparison, if measuring the savings from fusion, is ordinary bootstrapping followed by a separate evaluator for the same function at matched error and output level count.

Hard ReLU degree sweep¶

Extended the functional bootstrap comparison to eight Fourier degrees from 9 to 325 under the same single-threaded OpenFHE 1.6.1 configuration. Reused the three-sample results for degrees 45 and 129. Each new degree used one warm-up and two measured calls. Every result retained eight effective output levels.

The depth budget rose from 19 to 24. Degrees 257 and 325 both used 24 levels and took 25.59 s and 29.11 s, respectively. The ciphertext results matched the same-grid plaintext error summaries to within 1e-7. Deriving the exact plaintext Fourier tail and checking a 32,769-point grid showed that the original 1,024 slots miss the error peak at the upper boundary. Degree 129 has a slot maximum of 0.01266 and an interval supremum of 0.02513. With this extension on [-8, 8), degree 325 is the first to put the approximation supremum below 0.01. The ciphertext numerical error is a separate contribution.

Added the sweep, raw timings, exact error formula and depth thresholds to the implementation study. A follow-up at the same error budget could measure how changing the extension outside the target interval affects the required degree and latency.
